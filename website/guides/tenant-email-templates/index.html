Tenant email templates for property managers

Short, plain replies for the emails you answer every week. Copy them, change the details and send.

By Harleen Ghuman, founder of Clerova · November 2026

Each one covers a common email: a repair, an emergency, a question about a fee, a complaint or a move-out. They avoid promising times or outcomes you haven't confirmed yet. Rules on notices, fees and deposits differ by state and by lease, so check your own lease and local rules before you send anything about them.

The templates

Maintenance request received

Hi [first name], Thanks for letting us know about [the issue] at [address and unit]. We've logged it as a maintenance request, and our team will follow up to arrange the repair. If it gets worse before then, reply to this email right away.

Emergency: gas smell

Hi [first name], Please leave the building now and call 911 and your gas company's emergency line from outside. Don't use light switches, appliances or anything that could make a spark on your way out. We've marked this as an emergency and our team is on it. Reply to let us know you're safe.

Emergency: water leak or flooding

Hi [first name], Thanks for telling us right away. If you can do it safely, turn off the water valve for that fixture, and keep water away from outlets and appliances. We've logged this as urgent and our team will follow up as soon as possible.

Asking for missing details

Hi [first name], Thanks for reaching out. So we can get this fixed quickly, please reply with: - your unit number - a photo of the problem, if you can - whether we may enter if no one is home, and any pets we should know about Once we have that, we'll arrange the next step.

Question about a late fee

Hi [first name], Thanks for flagging this. We'll check your account and the payment date, and follow up with what we find. You don't need to send anything else for now.

Noise complaint

Hi [first name], Thank you for letting us know, and sorry it's been disruptive. We've passed this to our team, who will look into it and follow up. If it keeps happening, please reply with the dates and times so we have a clear record.

Move-out notice received

Hi [first name], Thanks for your note. We've received your notice to move out of [address and unit] on [date]. Our team will follow up with the move-out steps and what to expect for your deposit.

Friendly reminder when you need an answer

Hi [first name], Just checking in on our last email about [the issue]. When you have a moment, please reply with [what you need], and we'll take it from there. Thanks!

Tips for writing your own

  • Use the resident's first name and the exact address and unit.
  • Say what happens next, not what might happen.
  • Don't promise a time, a vendor or a refund until it's confirmed.
  • Give safety steps only for real danger: gas, fire, flooding, no heat in the cold.
  • Keep it short. Three short paragraphs is plenty.
